Validating Beneath the Skin against human biology

The project's question has changed. It now asks whether the simulator's internal muscle activations and tissue trajectories match measurements from real people, and surface plausibility is no longer the target. This plan reassesses the earlier ideas, checks what the datasets on the Ziva machine and the public MRI and articulography corpora can actually test, and lays out the work week by week through December.

1The short version

The simulator has no activation signal yet

The solver drives five structures: the jaw, the orbicularis oris, both orbicularis oculi and the frontalis. Its Hill-type muscle model runs with a fixed activation of 0.8. Zygomaticus major, levator labii, the depressors, mentalis and the other modeled muscles move only passively through Ziva attachments. Comparing activations with EMG therefore starts with defining what counts as activation in the model.

Existing data covers most of the questions

Public and on-machine data can test which muscles each expression recruits, how the lips and jaw move in speech, how far people coarticulate, and how much the zygomaticus major shortens and thickens in a smile.

It cannot cover the central one

No dataset records muscle activity, internal muscle deformation and 3D surface motion on the same people performing the simulator's own poses and sentences. That is the gap an in-vivo study has to close.

Recommendation

Run a validation audit on existing data in weeks 4 to 10. Draft the in-vivo protocol alongside it, because ethics review takes weeks. Propose making activation explicit in the solver as the follow-on contribution, once the audit shows where the model departs from biology.

Preliminary verdict Q1 recruitment: yes Q2 muscle paths: partly Q3 kinematics: yes Q4 jaw-lip timing: yes Q5 coarticulation: likely Q6 effort: partly Same-subject multimodal: no

Gap 1 · activation

Activation is a constant

Every code path that computes muscle force sets α = 0.8. Force then varies only through the force-length term, which the paper parameterizes by the remaining distance to the target, and it is clamped at 17/300 N per landmark. Muscle work therefore mostly restates how far the landmarks moved.

Gap 2 · prime movers

The muscles that do the work in people are passive here

The scene has about two dozen muscle meshes, but the solver actuates five structures. A smile (AU12) is made by moving orbicularis oris landmarks, and the zygomaticus major follows through the modiolus node. In people the zygomaticus major is the prime mover for AU12.

Gap 3 · time

Time is the optimizer's time

Frames are solver steps played back at 30 fps under velocity caps taken from chewing studies. Order, overlap and trajectory shape can be compared directly. Absolute speeds and durations need the caps checked first.

One modeling choice helps. The lips are a single volume of muscle, fat and skin. Midsagittal MRI also shows the lip as one block of soft tissue, so real-time MRI of speech is a fair comparison for the lips.

4Validation questions

Six questions, each tied to a claim in the paper and to data that can test it. The IDs are used in the plan in section 8.

This has been done before at smaller scale. Lucero and Munhall (1999) drove a face model with Hill-type muscles from recorded perioral EMG and compared it with marker kinematics. Eskes et al. (2018) drove a generic finite-element face with person-specific sEMG in six volunteers and reproduced measured 3D marker motion with a median correlation around 0.77 to 0.78. Nazari et al. built a speech face model with muscles for the same purpose. Those studies run forward, from EMG to motion. This project starts at the other end, from a target pose to an implied activation, which is why Q1 compares recruitment patterns instead of driving the model with EMG.

Q1

Recruitment: does the model drive the muscles people use?

Claim tested: the motor controllers translate artistic intent into muscle activations.

Compare
For each pose, which structures the solver drives and how much muscle work each does, against normalized sEMG per electrode site.
Data
Mimics and Muscles (main), Mimetic Interfaces (check), the Jena atlas papers for published group patterns.
Metrics
Agonist agreement (is the most active human site over a driven structure?), Spearman ρ between the model's effort vector and the EMG vector across sites, and the number of poses whose human prime mover is passive in the model.
Prediction
Fails for AU12, AU12L and AU6+12+25ii, where the zygomaticus major is passive, and for AU4, where corrugator, procerus and depressor supercilii are passive or absent. Should pass for AU1, AU2 and AU22.
Effort
About 2 weeks including the EMG pipeline.
Q2

Muscle paths: do muscle geometry and passive deformation match anatomy?

Claim tested: the model simulates internal anatomy well enough for biomechanical and medical analysis.

Compare
Rest geometry of the zygomaticus major and orbicularis oris (origin, insertion, length, angle, thickness) against anatomical and MRI measurements. Zygomaticus major length and thickness change in AU12 against MRI of a held smile.
Data
The zygomaticus functional MRI study (34 adults; in joy, length 85.6% and thickness 108.4% of rest), Happak et al. 1997 for facial muscle dimensions, real-time MRI lip sections for the lumped orbicularis oris.
Metrics
Each model value as a z-score within the human distribution. Inside the 95% prediction interval counts as a pass.
Caveat
Passing Q2 does not establish activation. A passively dragged zygomaticus can shorten and thicken like an active one, and only Q1 can tell them apart. Read Q1 and Q2 together.
Effort
About 1 week once the export layer exists.
Q3

Kinematics: do lip and jaw trajectories look like human speech movements?

Claim tested: the trajectories are biomechanically accurate and respect human velocity limits.

Compare
Velocity-profile shape (time-to-peak fraction, skew), peak speed against movement amplitude, peak speeds and durations for jaw opening (AA) and lip closure and rounding (V, UW).
Data
HPRC EMA, USC-TIMIT, the USC 75-speaker real-time MRI with Annot-16 contours, the French real-time MRI set.
Metrics
Shape distance on normalized time, the model's peak-speed-to-amplitude slope against the human regression, and the share of human movements faster than the model's caps.
Prediction
Human jaw speeds in speech will sit near or above the caps. Speech runs at roughly 4 to 5 syllables per second, so each opening has about 100 to 125 ms. The rotational cap matters most for speech, and its 17 cm radius assumption keeps it low.
Effort
About 1 week.
Q4

Jaw-lip coordination: does the 85% slowdown rule match human timing?

Claim tested: the paper itself says in vivo data is needed to confirm the jaw's synchronized deceleration.

Compare
Relative timing of jaw and lip peak velocity and target arrival in bilabial closures and rounding transitions, and the share of jaw movement spent decelerating.
Data
HPRC EMA. Masapollo et al. 2025 report tight jaw-lip peak-velocity coupling during closing and looser coupling during opening, with a supplement on ASHA figshare. Real-time MRI as a second source.
Metric
Arrival-time difference, jaw minus lip, for the model against the human distribution.
Effort
About 1 week, sharing code with Q3.
Q5

Coarticulation: does controller overlap match human anticipation?

Claim tested: overlapping controllers reproduce human coarticulation (10% and 40% overlaps were shown).

Compare
The model's overlap fraction against human anticipatory onset of the next gesture: orbicularis oris EMG onset before rounded vowels (UW in "you" and "new") and lip-protrusion onset in real-time MRI, both relative to phone boundaries.
Data
The speech EMG set on the Ziva machine if it has English audio and lip channels, otherwise Gowda et al. 2024. Real-time MRI with Annot-16 phone alignment. The classic EMG work on anticipatory lip rounding (Bell-Berti and Harris) for reference values.
Metric
Onset lead in ms and as a fraction of the preceding segment, compared with the 10% and 40% settings.
Reuse
The forced-alignment pipeline from the VOCASET branch.
Effort
About 2 weeks.
Q6

Effort: does the model's energy ranking match human effort?

Claim tested: the simulation reflects the energy parsimony of speech.

Compare
The rank of muscle work and strain energy across the 19 precomputed transitions against the rank of integrated EMG for the same transitions, and whether overlap lowers effort in both.
Caveat
The model's muscle work is roughly displacement times a near-constant force, so normalize both sides by movement amplitude before comparing.
Data
Speech EMG with phone alignment.
Effort
About 1 week. Stretch goal.

Mimics and Muscles

Strongest for expressions

Jena University Hospital and Jena computer vision group · access by agreement

Source
Büchner, Anders, Guntinas-Lichius and Denzler, EIFER (CVPR 2025). Same cohort as Mueller et al. 2022.
People
36 adults, 19 women and 17 men, aged 18 to 67.
Signals
Bilateral sEMG in two schemes at once. Fridlund: 22 bipolar channels (frontalis F1-F4, glabella F5-F8, levator labii superioris F9-F10, orbicularis oris F11-F12, depressor anguli oris F13-F14, mentalis F15-F16, orbicularis oculi F17-F18, zygomaticus F19-F20, masseter F21-F22). Kuramoto: a monopolar grid. 4096 Hz. Frontal video at 30 fps, trigger-synced.
Tasks
Rest, forehead raise, gentle and tight eye closure, closed and open smile, nose wrinkle, cheek blow, lip pucker, snarl, lower-lip depression, and six basic emotions. Two sessions with electrodes and one without, two weeks apart.
Fits poses
AU0, AU1 and AU2, AU6 and AU7, AU12, AU6+12+25ii, AU22. A pucker is closer to AU18, so compare it with the artist's AU22 sculpt before pairing them.
Limits
Electrodes cover the face, the video is 2D, and there is no speech. Not every participant consented to reuse, so the copy on the Ziva machine may be a subset. Next step
Read the data agreement before copying anything off the Ziva machine. Check the dataset's own channel map: the paper's appendix puts depressor anguli oris on F19-F20, while its Table 3 puts it on F13-F14.

EnoughPublic and on-machine data can carry

  • Q1 for expressions, with Mimics and Muscles as the main set and Mimetic Interfaces as a check.
  • Q3 and Q4, with EMA and real-time MRI.
  • The zygomaticus part of Q2, from the MRI study's group values.
  • Q5 and Q6, if the speech EMG set has audio and lip channels. Otherwise Gowda et al. or real-time MRI alone.

Not enoughWhat still needs in-vivo data

  • Muscle activity, internal deformation and 3D surface recorded on the same people.
  • Small or deep muscles (risorius, buccinator, levator anguli oris, depressor labii inferioris), where surface EMG mixes neighbours.
  • 3D dynamic deformation of facial muscles. The closest public evidence is static MRI of held expressions.
  • Upright posture. MRI is supine, which flips the gravity direction behind the paper's sag effects.
  • The model's exact 20 poses and sentences. Every dataset has its own task list.
  • Most of what speech MRI images (tongue, velum, larynx) is not actuated in the model. Only the lips and jaw are usable.

A channel is a site, not a muscle. Facial muscles are small and overlapping, so each electrode picks up its neighbours. Lapatki et al. (2010) derived better bipolar positions for the lower face from motor-unit maps. Treat each channel as a weighted mix of nearby muscles.

Virtual electrodes. Place the Fridlund sites on the registered skin mesh and compute, for each site, a distance-weighted sum of the simulated activity of nearby muscles. Model and human can then be compared channel by channel with crosstalk included. With today's model every passive muscle contributes zero, which is exactly what Q1 should expose.

9In-vivo protocol outline

Aim. On the same people, measure muscle activity, internal muscle deformation and surface motion while they perform the model's own poses and sentences. Reconcile this outline with the proj-b1 post before circulating it.

People
12 healthy adults for a pilot, balanced by sex. That sits within comparable studies (6 in Eskes et al. 2018, 34 in the zygomaticus MRI study, 36 in the Jena cohort) and is enough to estimate the between-person spread used as the pass band. Exclude facial neuromuscular disorders, facial surgery and botulinum toxin in the past six months. Clean-shaven at the electrode sites, as in the Jena study. MRI safety screening for the MRI arm.
Tasks
The AU poses from the paper's Table 1, cued by video, five repetitions each with a paced 1 s rise, 2 s hold and 1 s release. The eight phonemes in VCV frames, and the model's sentences ("Not even you", "You've not eaten it yet", "New tea, new yacht, new TV") at normal and slow rates. Jaw opening and closing at three speeds, for the velocity caps. Maximum voluntary contractions per muscle, for normalization.

B. Ultrasound, about 30 min B-mode ultrasound over the zygomaticus major and orbicularis oris, synced with sEMG: thickness and displacement, upright. Q2 dynamically, and active against passive shortening in the same trial. Low-medium Needs an ultrasound with a linear probe and a trained operator.
C. MRI session, about 60 min 3D T1 and T2 head scans at rest (subject anatomy, not defaced), static 3D holds for AU12, UW and AA, real-time 2D in the midsagittal plane and an oblique plane along the zygomaticus. Q2 in 3D, internal lip and jaw trajectories. High MRI time, a physicist for real-time sequences, supine posture.
Sync
Lab Streaming Layer for EMG, audio and video in the lab session, as in Gowda et al. and Shu et al. Standard EMG cannot run inside the scanner without MR-compatible hardware, so plan MRI and EMG as separate sessions and align them by task and repetition, not by clock.
Analysis
Reuse the audit pipelines. Each participant gets a comparison band, and the model is scored per person and for the group.
Logistics
  • Ethics application: consent for face video and MRI, consent tiers for data sharing (the Jena dataset shows the cost of not having them), an incidental-findings policy for MRI.
  • MRI centre: scan time and a physicist for real-time sequences.
  • Equipment: an EMG amplifier with 16 or more bipolar channels, facial electrodes, an ultrasound with a linear probe, a synced camera rig or depth sensor, a microphone.
  • People: an ultrasound operator and a second rater for MRI measurements.
  • Timing: ethics review can take several weeks, so submit early next term at the latest.

11Methods notes

What sEMG measures
Surface EMG amplitude reflects the summed activity of many motor units, so recruitment and firing rate are mixed together. Firing rates need high-density decomposition or needle EMG. Envelopes are the right observable here because the model has no motor units.
Preprocessing
Band-pass 20 to 450 Hz, a notch at the mains frequency and its harmonics, full-wave rectification, then 125 ms RMS windows (as in Mueller et al. 2022) or a 6 to 10 Hz low-pass envelope. Normalize per person to maximum voluntary contraction, or to the maximum across tasks as EIFER did.
Onsets
Envelope above the rest mean plus 3 SD for at least 25 ms, from the Hodges and Bui (1996) family of detectors. Report the detector and its settings.
Crosstalk
Compare at the site level, use virtual electrodes for the model side, and check placements against Lapatki et al. (2010).
Time
Compare trajectory shapes on normalized time (0 = onset, 1 = target reached). Compare order and overlap directly. Compare absolute speeds only after the velocity caps are checked.
Statistics
Use the human between-person spread as the reference band (95% prediction interval). Use mixed models with a random intercept per person for repeated measures, as Mueller et al. (2022) did. Report effect sizes and keep the thresholds agreed in advance.
Units
1 Maya unit = 0.0962 cm (Appendix B). Store all exports in millimetres.
Data handling
Keep agreement-restricted data on the Ziva machine. Commit loaders and derived, non-identifying tables only. Keep the VOCASET branches local as before.
